## 1. Executive Summary

This report establishes a systematic quality-review process for the Curious Mind — AI Overview educational project.

The project aims to introduce beginner learners to artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning, generative AI, practical applications, responsible AI use, and practical AI skills through structured learning materials and supporting resources.

The audit focuses on two essential assignment requirements:

1. **Complete content structure:** The module outline must describe the overall course, its learning objectives, the sequence of modules and chapters, and the intended learning activities and assessments.
2. **At least one fully developed demonstration chapter:** At least one chapter must demonstrate the intended teaching approach through detailed explanations, examples, learning activities, assessments, answer explanations where appropriate, and references.

The audit also examines supporting materials, consistency across files, source verification, accessibility, collaboration records, and final submission readiness.

**Important status statement:** This report defines the checks and records their results. No item should be marked as passed until the corresponding file or evidence has actually been inspected.

## 4. Audit Methodology

The audit should be conducted using the following process.

### Step 1: Inspect the Repository

- Check that expected files are present.
- Confirm that files are located in the intended directories.
- Open and inspect the contents of each required file.
- Identify missing, duplicated, empty, or outdated documents.

### Step 2: Review Essential Deliverables

Inspect `content/00-module-outline.md` and the selected demonstration chapter in detail.

Compare both documents with the assignment instructions and the available marking rubric.

### Step 3: Review Supporting Materials

Check whether the study guide, practice questions, presentation, lecture script, video materials, and other supporting documents align with the course outline and learning objectives.

### Step 4: Check Accuracy and Accessibility

Verify important claims and references, inspect links, and check that the materials use appropriate language, structure, and accessibility practices.

### Step 5: Record Evidence and Findings

For each audit criterion, record:

- The file or section inspected.
- The evidence observed.
- The status of the criterion.
- Any identified issue.
- The corrective action required.
- The reviewer or person responsible, where applicable.

### Step 6: Verify Corrections

Recheck revised files before changing an audit status to **Met** or **Pass**.

### 4.1 Audit Status Definitions

Use these status labels consistently.

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Not reviewed | The relevant file or criterion has not yet been inspected. |
| Needs revision | An issue prevents the criterion from being satisfied. |
| Partially met | Some requirements are satisfied, but others remain incomplete. |
| Met | The criterion has been checked and supported by sufficient evidence. |
| Not applicable | The criterion does not apply, with a brief justification recorded. |

Do not use "Met" merely because a file exists or appears complete at first glance.

## 9. Accessibility and Learning Design Audit

**Status:** Not reviewed

### 9.1 Accessibility Criteria

Check whether:

- Headings follow a logical hierarchy.
- Technical terms are defined clearly.
- Paragraphs are readable and appropriately structured.
- Links use meaningful descriptive text.
- Informative images have useful alternative text.
- Decorative visuals do not distract from learning.
- Tables are understandable and not unnecessarily complex.
- Important information is not conveyed through color alone.
- Examples are inclusive and avoid unnecessary stereotypes.
- Activities explain what learners must do and how their work will be evaluated.
- Video materials include captions or a transcript where required or appropriate.
- Presentation text is readable and visual contrast is adequate.

### 9.2 Acceptance Criteria

Accessibility issues should be corrected or documented before submission, with priority given to barriers that prevent learners from accessing essential content.

A checklist alone is not proof of accessibility. Inspect the actual rendered materials.
