
# Chapter 3: Generative AI and Large Language Models (LLMs)

## 3.1 Introduction

Generative artificial intelligence (generative AI) is a type of AI that can create new content, such as text, images, audio, video, and computer code. These systems learn patterns from training data and use those patterns to generate new outputs in response to instructions or prompts.

Large language models (LLMs) are a type of AI model designed to process and generate human language. They can help users summarise information, answer questions, draft text, translate languages, and generate computer code.

Generative AI tools are increasingly used in education, business, software development, and creative work. However, their outputs are not always accurate, unbiased, or appropriate. Understanding how these systems work and how to evaluate their responses is essential for responsible use.

## 3.3 What Is Generative AI?

Generative AI refers to AI systems that produce new content based on patterns learned from training data.

Depending on the system, the generated content may include:

- Text, such as explanations, summaries, and stories.
- Images, such as illustrations and concept art.
- Audio, such as speech or music.
- Video, such as short generated clips.
- Computer code, such as example functions or scripts.

Generative AI does not necessarily understand information in the same way that a human does. It generates outputs based on learned patterns and the instructions it receives.

**Example:**

A user enters the prompt, “Explain artificial intelligence in simple language for a beginner.”

A generative AI tool may produce a short explanation using accessible vocabulary and examples. The user can then request a shorter explanation, ask for an analogy, or request a quiz to check their understanding.

**Key point:** Generative AI creates content, but the generated content should be checked before it is trusted or used.

## 3.4 Generative AI and Traditional AI

AI systems can be designed for different purposes. Some systems primarily classify information or make predictions, while generative systems create new content.

| AI task | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Classification | Assign an item to a category | Classifying an email as spam or not spam |
| Prediction | Estimate a likely value or outcome | Predicting future sales from historical data |
| Recommendation | Suggest relevant items | Recommending films based on viewing history |
| Generation | Create new content | Drafting a story or generating an image |

These categories can overlap. For example, a generative AI system may also use classification or prediction components.

The main difference is the intended output: a classification system assigns a label, while a generative system produces content.

## 3.5 What Are Large Language Models?

A large language model (LLM) is an AI model trained on large amounts of text and other language data. It learns statistical patterns and relationships between words, tokens, and pieces of text.

LLMs can perform tasks such as:

- Answering questions.
- Summarising documents.
- Drafting emails and reports.
- Explaining unfamiliar concepts.
- Translating text.
- Helping with computer programming.
- Generating ideas and outlines.

Some LLMs can also process images or other types of input. Their capabilities depend on the model and the tools connected to it.

**What is a token?**

A token is a unit of text processed by a language model. A token may represent a word, part of a word, punctuation, or another text unit. The exact tokenisation depends on the model.

## 3.6 How Do LLMs Generate Text?

Many LLMs generate text by repeatedly estimating which token should come next, given the preceding context.

A simplified process is:

1. Receive a prompt from the user.
2. Process the input as tokens.
3. Estimate possible next tokens.
4. Select a token according to the model's generation settings.
5. Repeat the process until the response is complete.
6. Return the generated response to the user.

Actual systems may include additional components, such as safety controls, retrieval systems, tools, or image-processing capabilities.

**Important limitation:** Generating a plausible sentence does not guarantee that it is factually correct. A language model can produce fluent text that contains mistakes, misleading statements, or invented details.

Users should verify important claims using reliable sources.

## 3.7 Common Applications of Generative AI

### Education

Generative AI can help learners understand difficult concepts, generate practice questions, create revision summaries, and explore alternative explanations. Learners should follow their institution's rules about AI use.

### Business

Businesses may use generative AI to draft routine communications, summarise documents, brainstorm ideas, prepare initial reports, and support customer-service workflows. Human review remains important.

### Healthcare

Generative AI may support certain administrative and information tasks. Its use in healthcare requires appropriate safeguards, expert oversight, and attention to privacy. AI-generated health information should not replace professional medical advice.

### Software Development

Developers may use generative AI to suggest code, explain unfamiliar code, generate tests, identify possible errors, and draft technical documentation. Generated code should be reviewed, tested, and checked for security problems.

### Creative Work

Generative AI can assist with brainstorming, image creation, story outlines, music experiments, and design concepts. Users should consider copyright, permissions, attribution, and the rights of other creators.

## 3.8 Benefits of Generative AI

Generative AI can provide several benefits when used appropriately.

1. **Faster first drafts:** It can help produce initial drafts of text, plans, and summaries.
2. **Support for learning:** It can explain concepts in different ways and generate practice questions.
3. **Idea generation:** It can suggest approaches for projects and problem-solving.
4. **Assistance with repetitive tasks:** It can help with routine writing and summarisation.
5. **Adaptable responses:** Users can request different levels of detail, tones, or formats.

These benefits depend on the quality of the prompt, the capabilities of the system, and the user's ability to evaluate the output.

## 3.9 Limitations and Risks

### Inaccurate Information

A system may provide incorrect facts or explanations while sounding confident.

### Hallucinations

An AI hallucination is an output that presents unsupported or fabricated information as though it were true. For example, a model might invent a research paper or provide a reference that does not exist.

### Bias

Models may reproduce unfair stereotypes or patterns found in their training data.

### Outdated Information

A model may not have access to the latest information unless it uses an appropriate retrieval or browsing system.

### Privacy Risks

Entering personal, confidential, or sensitive information into an AI tool may create privacy risks. Users should follow the tool's privacy settings and their organisation's data-handling rules.

### Overreliance

Relying on generated answers without independent thought or verification can weaken learning and lead to poor decisions.

### Copyright and Ownership Questions

The use of AI-generated content may raise questions about copyright, permission, attribution, and ownership. Relevant rules depend on the circumstances and applicable law.

## 3.10 Responsible Use of Generative AI

Responsible use means using AI in ways that are safe, transparent, fair, and appropriate for the task.

Good practices include:

1. Verify important claims using reliable sources.
2. Protect privacy and avoid entering confidential information without authorisation.
3. Review responses for possible bias.
4. Disclose AI assistance when required.
5. Respect intellectual property and copyright rules.
6. Keep human oversight for important decisions.
7. Follow academic integrity rules and course policies.

Generative AI should support human judgement rather than replace responsibility for the final result.

## 3.11 Writing Effective Prompts

A prompt is the instruction or input given to an AI system.

A clear prompt often includes:

- **Task:** What should the AI do?
- **Context:** What background information does it need?
- **Audience:** Who is the response for?
- **Format:** Should the response be a list, table, or paragraph?
- **Constraints:** What should the response include or avoid?

**Weak prompt:**

“Explain AI.”

**Improved prompt:**

“Explain artificial intelligence to a beginner in about 150 words. Include two everyday examples, use simple language, and finish with three key points.”

The improved prompt provides a clearer task and more useful constraints.

If the first response is not suitable, the user can refine the prompt by asking for a shorter explanation, more examples, simpler language, or a different format.

Prompt refinement can improve relevance, but it does not guarantee accuracy.

## 3.12 Practical Activity: Improve and Evaluate a Prompt

**Activity purpose:**

Practise writing a clear prompt and evaluating an AI-generated response.

**Instructions:**

1. Choose a topic related to AI, such as machine learning, image recognition, or chatbots.
2. Write a simple prompt asking an AI tool to explain the topic.
3. Record the response.
4. Improve the prompt by specifying the audience, desired length, and format.
5. Compare the two responses.
6. Identify at least one claim that should be verified.
7. Check that claim against a reliable source.
8. Write a short reflection on which prompt was more useful and why.

**Example initial prompt:**

“Explain machine learning.”

**Example improved prompt:**

“Explain machine learning to a beginner in approximately 120 words. Include one everyday example, avoid technical jargon, and finish with two review questions.”

**Questions to consider:**

- Which prompt produced the more useful response?
- Was the explanation understandable?
- Did the response contain any unsupported claims?
- What information needed verification?
- What would you change in the prompt next time?

**Expected outcome:**

Learners should demonstrate that clear instructions can improve the relevance of an AI response and that generated information still requires evaluation.

## 3.14 Chapter Summary

Generative AI systems create new content based on patterns learned from training data. Large language models are designed to process and generate language and can support tasks such as summarisation, drafting, translation, and explanation.

Many LLMs generate text by repeatedly predicting likely next tokens based on context. This process can produce useful responses, but it does not guarantee that the information is correct.

Generative AI offers benefits in education, business, software development, and creative work. However, users must consider inaccurate information, hallucinations, bias, privacy, copyright, and overreliance.

Effective prompts provide clear tasks, context, audience, format, and constraints. Responsible users verify important claims, protect sensitive information, follow relevant rules, and maintain human oversight.
