# Chapter 07: Covering Symbolic AI

## 1. Overview

Symbolic AI, also known as Good Old-Fashioned AI (GOFAI), focuses on high-level symbolic reasoning and the manipulation of symbols to simulate human intelligence. It relies on explicit rules and logic to represent knowledge and make decisions.

## 2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, learners should be able to:

- Explain the concept of Symbolic AI.
- Understand logic and reasoning.
- Describe knowledge representation.
- Explain rule-based systems and expert systems.
- Understand planning and problem-solving.
- Identify applications of Natural Language Processing (NLP) in Symbolic AI.

## 3. Introduction to Symbolic AI

Symbolic AI uses symbols, facts, rules, and logical expressions to represent knowledge. It processes this information through reasoning mechanisms to reach conclusions or make decisions.

For example, a medical diagnosis system may use predefined rules to examine symptoms and suggest possible conditions.

## 4. Logic and Reasoning

Logic and reasoning allow AI systems to draw conclusions from available information.

### 4.1 Propositional Logic

Propositional logic deals with statements that are either true or false.

Examples:

- The sky is blue.
- It is raining.
- 2 + 2 = 4.

The three fundamental Boolean operators are:

- **AND (∧):** True only when both propositions are true.
- **OR (∨):** True when at least one proposition is true.
- **NOT (¬):** Reverses the truth value.

### 4.2 Predicate Logic

Predicate logic uses predicates, variables, and quantifiers to represent more complex statements.

Two important quantifiers are:

- **Universal quantifier (∀):** Expresses that something applies to all elements.
- **Existential quantifier (∃):** Expresses that at least one element satisfies a condition.

## 5. Knowledge Representation

Knowledge representation organizes information so an AI system can use it to reason and solve problems.

Common methods include:

- Logic-based representation
- Semantic networks
- Frames and scripts
- Rule-based representation
- Ontologies

## 6. Rule-Based Systems and Expert Systems

Rule-based systems use conditional IF-THEN statements to make decisions.

Example:

IF the temperature is greater than 30°C, THEN turn on the air conditioning.

Expert systems imitate the decision-making ability of human experts in specific fields.

Their main components include:

- Knowledge base
- Inference engine
- User interface

## 7. Planning and Problem Solving

Planning determines a sequence of actions to reach a goal.

Important concepts include:

- State space
- Actions
- Goal state
- Search algorithms

Examples of search algorithms include:

- Breadth-First Search (BFS)
- Depth-First Search (DFS)
- Uniform-Cost Search
- A* Search
- Greedy Best-First Search
- Minimax Algorithm

## 8. Natural Language Processing in Symbolic AI

Symbolic NLP uses formal rules, logic, and symbolic representations to process human language.

It involves:

- Syntax and semantics
- Knowledge representation
- Rule-based language processing
- Logic and inference
- Sentiment analysis
- Question-answering systems
- Information retrieval

## 9. Applications of Symbolic AI

Symbolic AI can be applied in:

- Medical diagnosis systems
- Expert systems
- Natural language processing
- Robotics
- Decision-support systems

## 10. Benefits and Limitations

### Benefits

- Interpretability: Decisions can be traced through explicit rules.
- Knowledge representation: Information can be organized in structured forms.
- Flexibility: Rules can be modified for different domains.

### Limitations

- Requires well-defined knowledge.
- Can become computationally expensive as rules increase.
- Struggles with uncertain or ambiguous information.
- Has limited ability to learn and adapt automatically.

## 11. Chapter Summary

Symbolic AI represents knowledge using symbols, facts, rules, and logic. It supports reasoning, planning, and problem-solving through structured knowledge representation. Although it is interpretable and useful in rule-based applications, it can struggle with uncertainty and learning from new data.
