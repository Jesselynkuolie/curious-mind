## 1. Using AI as a Support Tool

Artificial intelligence (AI) can help learners brainstorm ideas, organize information, explain difficult concepts, practice skills, and receive feedback. However, AI should support the learning process rather than replace independent thinking, personal effort, or responsibility.

For example, a student preparing for a machine-learning examination can ask an AI tool to generate practice questions. The student should then attempt the questions independently, check the explanations against course materials, and identify areas that need further study.

AI-generated content may contain incorrect information, incomplete explanations, outdated facts, or biased assumptions. Therefore, users should treat AI responses as starting points for investigation rather than automatically accepting them as facts.

**Key principle:** Use AI to improve your understanding, not to avoid developing your own understanding.

## 2. Writing a Clear Prompt

A prompt is the instruction or question given to an AI tool. A clear prompt helps the tool produce a response that better matches the user's needs. However, even a well-written prompt does not guarantee that the response will be correct.

An effective prompt can include five components:

1. Task: What should the AI do?
2. Context: What background information does it need?
3. Audience: Who is the response intended for?
4. Format: How should the answer be organized?
5. Constraints: What limits or requirements should it follow?
6. Not every prompt needs all five components. Their usefulness depends on the task.
### Example: Improving a prompt

Initial prompt:

"Help me study machine learning."

This prompt is vague because it does not specify the topics, difficulty level, audience, or desired output.

Improved prompt:

"Create five beginner-level practice questions about supervised and unsupervised learning for a high-school student who is new to machine learning. Present the questions in a numbered list, then provide an answer key with short explanations in simple English. Keep each explanation under three sentences."

### The improved prompt is more specific because it defines:
| Component | Example in the prompt |
|---|---|
| Task | Create five practice questions |
| Context | Supervised and unsupervised learning |
| Audience | A high-school student new to machine learning |
| Format | Numbered questions followed by an answer key |
| Constraints | Simple English and explanations under three sentences |

### Practical tips for prompt writing:

- Use specific instructions instead of broad requests.

- Provide relevant background information.

- State the expected output format.

- Set appropriate limits, such as length, language, or difficulty.

- Refine the prompt when the first response does not meet your needs.

- Avoid including passwords, private records, or confidential information.

## 3. Evaluating AI Answers

An AI response may sound confident and well organized while still containing errors. Before using its output, evaluate it using the following checklist.

| Criterion | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Are the facts, definitions, calculations, and examples correct? |
| Relevance | Does the response directly answer the question? |
| Completeness | Does it cover the important points without leaving out necessary information? |
| Clarity | Is the explanation understandable and logically organized? |
| Bias | Does it present assumptions or viewpoints unfairly? |
| Currency | Is the information up to date enough for this task? |
| Sources | Are the sources identifiable, reliable, and relevant to the claims? |

The importance of each criterion depends on the task. For example, currency is especially important when researching current regulations, statistics, or technology developments. Accuracy and completeness are essential when learning a new concept or solving a mathematical problem.

### Worked example: Evaluating a machine-learning claim

Suppose an AI tool gives the following statement:

"All machine-learning systems learn without labels."

**Step 1: Identify the problem**

The statement uses the word "all," making an overly broad claim about machine learning.

**Step 2: Check the concept**

Machine learning includes different approaches. Supervised learning typically uses labeled examples, while unsupervised learning typically identifies patterns in data without provided labels.

**Step 3: Verify the explanation**

Compare the statement with reliable course materials or an authoritative machine-learning textbook or educational resource.

**Step 4: Correct the claim**

A more accurate version is:

"Machine-learning systems use different learning approaches. Supervised learning typically uses labeled training examples, whereas unsupervised learning typically works with data without provided labels to identify patterns."

**Step 5: Learn from the mistake**

The example demonstrates why users should examine absolute statements such as "all," "always," and "never." These words are not necessarily wrong, but they often require careful verification.
## 4. Verifying Sources

AI-generated answers may include inaccurate claims, outdated information, or references that do not support the statements they accompany. Source verification helps users distinguish reliable information from unsupported claims.
Follow these steps when checking an important claim:

- **Identify the exact claim:** Determine which fact, statistic, quotation, or explanation needs verification.
- **Find the original source:** Whenever possible, locate the original research paper, official report, dataset, regulation, or document.
- **Assess source reliability:** Consider the author's expertise, the publishing organization, the evidence provided, and the purpose of the publication.
- **Check the date and context:** Confirm that the information is current enough and applies to the situation being discussed.
- **Compare independent sources:** Check whether other credible sources support the same conclusion.
- **Confirm the evidence matches the claim:** A source may be legitimate but still fail to support the specific statement.

## 5. Example of Critical Evaluation

Suppose an AI tool states:
"AI adoption in China increased by 40% in 2025."
It also provides a link to a general article about artificial intelligence in China.

A responsible user should:

- Determine what "AI adoption" means in the claim.
- Identify the population, industry, measurement method, and time period.
- Find the original report or dataset behind the 40% figure.
- Check whether the source actually reports a 40% increase in 2025.
- Compare the figure with another credible source where possible.
- Remove or correct the statistic if it cannot be verified.

A general article about AI does not automatically prove a specific statistic. If the original evidence cannot be found, the claim should be described as unverified rather than presented as an established fact.

**Useful source types include:**

- Official government websites and statistical agencies.
- Peer-reviewed academic research.
- University publications and established educational resources.
- Original datasets, technical documentation, and official reports from relevant organizations.

The appropriate source depends on the claim. For example, an official government source may be suitable for a regulation, while an academic study may be more appropriate for a research finding.

## 6. Academic Responsibility

Responsible AI use requires users to follow academic rules, protect information, and remain accountable for their work.
### Good practices

- **Follow course policies:** Check whether AI is permitted for the specific assignment and what types of assistance are allowed.
- **Understand your work:** Be able to explain the reasoning, evidence, and conclusions in your submission.
- **Verify important information:** Check facts, calculations, quotations, and references before using them.
- **Maintain academic integrity:** Do not submit AI-generated work as entirely your own when doing so violates course requirements.
- **Disclose AI assistance when required:** Follow the instructor's preferred method for acknowledging AI use.
- **Protect privacy:** Do not enter personal data, passwords, confidential documents, or other sensitive information into unapproved tools.
- **Retain responsibility:** The user remains responsible for the accuracy, originality, and appropriateness of the final work.
## 7. Practical Activity: Use, Evaluate, and Improve

### Instructions

**Step 1: Choose a learning task**

Select a topic you are currently studying, such as machine learning, mathematics, economics, or academic writing.

**Step 2: Write a prompt**

Include the task, relevant context, intended audience, desired format, and any useful constraints.

**Step 3: Review the AI response**

Use the evaluation checklist from Section 3. Identify at least one strength and one potential weakness.

**Step 4: Verify a claim**

Select one factual claim from the response. Find a reliable source and check whether the evidence supports it.

**Step 5: Improve the result**

Correct unsupported or inaccurate information. Rewrite any explanation that remains unclear or incomplete.

**Step 6: Reflect on the process**

Answer the following questions:
- What task did you ask the AI to perform?
- What did you include in your prompt to make it specific?
- Which part of the response was useful, and why?
- Which claim did you verify, and what evidence did you find?
- What did you correct, remove, or clarify?
- What would you change about your prompt or verification process next time?

## 8. Key Takeaways

- AI can support brainstorming, explanation, practice, and organization, but it should not replace independent thinking.
- Clear prompts specify the task and provide relevant context, audience, format, and constraints.
- AI-generated content should be evaluated for accuracy, relevance, completeness, clarity, bias, currency, and source quality.
- Reliable verification involves checking original evidence, dates, context, and whether the source supports the exact claim.
- Responsible AI use includes following academic rules, protecting privacy, and acknowledging assistance when required.
- Effective AI use combines technical assistance with human judgment, critical thinking, and accountability.
