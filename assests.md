
# Curious Mind — Assets Guide

## 1. Purpose

The `assets/` directory stores the visual and audio resources used in the Curious Mind — AI Overview learning materials. These resources support explanations, improve learner engagement, and make complex AI concepts easier to understand.

All assets must be relevant to the learning objectives, accessible to learners, and used in accordance with their licence and attribution requirements.

## 2. Directory Structure

```text
assets/
├── README.md
├── asset-register.md
├── images/
├── diagrams/
├── audio/
└── captions/
```

### Directory descriptions

| Directory | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `images/` | Educational illustrations and relevant photographs |
| `diagrams/` | AI concepts, machine-learning workflows, and explanatory diagrams |
| `audio/` | Narration and other approved audio resources |
| `captions/` | Captions and transcripts for audio and video materials |
| `asset-register.md` | Records the source, licence, attribution, and use of each asset |

Only add files that are actually used or planned for the learning materials. Do not list nonexistent files as completed assets.

## 3. Asset Selection Principles

Every asset should meet the following principles:

- **Relevance:** Supports a chapter learning objective or explains an important concept.
- **Accuracy:** Represents AI concepts correctly and avoids misleading learners.
- **Accessibility:** Includes suitable alternative text for meaningful images and captions or transcripts for audio and video.
- **Copyright compliance:** Has a verified licence or documented permission for use.
- **Consistency:** Follows a clear visual style throughout the learning materials.
- **Quality:** Is readable, appropriately sized, and suitable for its intended use.

## 5. Copyright and Attribution

Before including an asset:

1. Identify the original creator or source.
2. Check the licence and whether educational or redistribution use is permitted.
3. Record the source URL and access date where appropriate.
4. Provide attribution in the asset register or beside the asset when required.
5. Record any modifications made to the original.
6. Obtain permission when required and retain evidence of that permission.

Potential sources for appropriately licensed resources include:

- [Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/)
- [Openverse](https://openverse.org/)
- [Material Symbols](https://fonts.google.com/icons)

Always verify the licence of the individual asset. The availability of a resource on a website does not automatically mean it is free to reuse.

## 6. Accessibility Requirements

- Provide meaningful alternative text for informative images.
- Mark purely decorative images appropriately when using a supported format.
- Use readable text sizes and sufficient colour contrast.
- Do not rely on colour alone to communicate information.
- Provide captions and transcripts for relevant audio and video content.
- Ensure diagrams remain understandable when viewed at normal screen size.
- Explain complex diagrams in the surrounding learning material.

## 7. File Naming and Organization

Use descriptive, consistent filenames in lowercase with hyphens.

Examples:

- `ai-learning-workflow.png`
- `machine-learning-process.svg`
- `ai-introduction-narration.mp3`
- `video-transcript.md`

Use appropriate formats, such as SVG for suitable scalable diagrams, PNG for diagrams requiring raster images, and MP3 for compressed audio. Ensure that files render correctly in the intended learning platform.

## 8. Asset Management Workflow

1. Identify where an asset supports a learning objective.
2. Create the asset or locate a suitable resource.
3. Verify accuracy, source, licence, and permissions.
4. Check accessibility and visual quality.
5. Save the file in the correct directory.
6. Record the asset in `asset-register.md`.
7. Review the asset in the relevant chapter, slides, or video.
8. Confirm that the final repository contains the referenced file.
