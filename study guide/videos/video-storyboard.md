# Curious Mind: Understanding Artificial Intelligence
## Video Storyboard

**Project:** Curious Mind — Introduction to Artificial Intelligence  
**File:** `videos/video-storyboard.md`  
**Format:** Educational explainer video  
**Target duration:** 4 minutes 10 seconds  
**Target audience:** Students and beginners learning about artificial intelligence  
**Style:** Clear narration, simple animations, diagrams, and practical examples  
**Purpose:** To introduce AI, explain its main concepts and applications, and encourage responsible use.

---

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of the video, viewers should be able to:

1. Explain what artificial intelligence (AI) is in simple terms.
2. Distinguish between AI, machine learning, and generative AI.
3. Identify examples of AI in everyday life.
4. Recognise important risks, including bias, privacy concerns, and inaccurate outputs.
5. Describe at least one way to use AI responsibly.

## 3. Detailed Storyboard

### Scene 1: Introduction

**Time:** 00:00–00:15  
**Duration:** 15 seconds

**Visuals:**
- Open with the title, "Curious Mind: Understanding Artificial Intelligence".
- Show a simple animated sequence of a smartphone, a laptop, a voice assistant, and a digital image.
- Display the Curious Mind project title and team or course details if required.

**Narration:**

"Artificial intelligence is becoming part of our everyday lives. From the recommendations we receive online to tools that generate text and images, AI is changing how we learn, work, and communicate. But what exactly is AI?"

**On-screen text:**
- Curious Mind
- Understanding Artificial Intelligence
- Discover how AI works and how to use it responsibly.

**Audio and transitions:**
- Use soft background music at a low volume.
- Fade in the title and transition smoothly into the next scene.

**Production notes:**
- Keep the title readable.
- Use clear, uncluttered visuals.
- Avoid suggesting that AI is human or can think exactly like a person.

---

### Scene 2: What Is Artificial Intelligence?

**Time:** 00:15–00:45  
**Duration:** 30 seconds

**Visuals:**
- Display a simple diagram showing AI systems receiving information and producing an output.
- Show examples such as a voice assistant responding to a question and a recommendation system suggesting a video.
- Highlight the input and output in the diagram.

**Narration:**

"Artificial intelligence, or AI, refers to computer systems designed to perform tasks that commonly involve abilities such as recognising patterns, understanding language, making predictions, or generating content. Different AI systems work in different ways. They do not automatically understand the world as humans do, and their outputs can be incorrect."

**On-screen text:**
- AI = Computer systems performing tasks associated with intelligent behaviour
- Input → AI system → Output
- AI can make mistakes.

**Learning point:**

AI can perform useful tasks, but its capabilities and limitations depend on how the system is designed and used.

**Audio and transitions:**
- Highlight each part of the input-process-output diagram as it is mentioned.
- Transition from general AI examples to a visual representation of data and patterns.

**Production notes:**
- Use everyday examples that are easy for beginners to recognise.
- Do not imply that every automated system uses AI.

---

### Scene 3: Understanding Machine Learning

**Time:** 00:45–01:20  
**Duration:** 35 seconds

**Visuals:**
- Show a collection of labelled pictures of cats and dogs.
- Animate a machine-learning model learning patterns from example images.
- Display a new image being classified by the trained model.
- Add a simple flow diagram.

**Narration:**

"Machine learning is one approach within artificial intelligence. Instead of relying only on manually written rules, a machine-learning system learns patterns from data. For example, a model can be trained using labelled pictures of cats and dogs. After training, it can predict which animal appears in a new picture. However, its prediction may be wrong, especially when the new image differs from its training examples."

**On-screen text:**

AI  
↓  
Machine Learning  
↓  
Learning patterns from data

Example:

Training images → Model training → Prediction

**Learning point:**

Machine learning uses data to learn patterns and make predictions. The quality and suitability of the training data matter.

**Audio and transitions:**
- Use a simple animation to show examples entering the training process.
- Highlight the prediction result without presenting it as guaranteed to be correct.

**Production notes:**
- Keep the diagram simple.
- Make clear that machine learning is a part of AI, not a synonym for all AI.

---

### Scene 4: Generative AI

**Time:** 01:20–02:00  
**Duration:** 40 seconds

**Visuals:**
- Show a user entering a prompt into an AI tool.
- Display example outputs: a short paragraph, an image, and a summary.
- Show the prompt on one side and the generated output on the other.
- Briefly highlight an example of an incorrect AI-generated statement.

**Narration:**

"Generative AI creates new content, such as text, images, audio, or computer code. Many generative AI tools respond to instructions called prompts. For example, a student might ask an AI tool to explain a scientific concept in simple language. The result can be a useful starting point, but it may contain mistakes, invented details, or outdated information. That is why generated content should be checked before it is trusted or shared."

**On-screen text:**
- Generative AI creates content.
- Prompt → AI tool → Generated output
- Useful does not always mean accurate.

**Learning point:**

Generative AI can support learning and creativity, but users should review and verify its outputs.

**Audio and transitions:**
- Animate the prompt and output appearing on screen.
- Use a brief highlight to draw attention to the incorrect example.
- Transition from the generated content to real-world applications.

**Production notes:**
- Use an original or clearly identified demonstration prompt.
- Do not present generated information as a verified fact without checking it.
- Avoid displaying personal or confidential information in the demonstration.

---

### Scene 5: AI in Everyday Life

**Time:** 02:00–02:45  
**Duration:** 45 seconds

**Visuals:**
Show four examples in sequence:

1. **Education:** A student uses an AI tool to practise a topic.
2. **Healthcare:** A medical professional reviews information from an AI-assisted system.
3. **Transportation:** A navigation application estimates travel time or suggests a route.
4. **Online services:** A recommendation system suggests content based on user activity.

**Narration:**

"AI is used in many areas of everyday life. In education, it can help learners practise concepts and explore explanations. In healthcare, AI-assisted systems can help professionals analyse information, although clinical decisions require appropriate human oversight. Navigation systems can estimate travel times and suggest routes. Online services may use recommendation systems to suggest content. These examples show how AI can support people, but its usefulness depends on the task, the data, and how carefully the results are evaluated."

**On-screen text:**
- Education: Learning support
- Healthcare: Decision support
- Transportation: Route recommendations
- Online services: Content recommendations

**Learning point:**

AI can assist people in different fields, but it should be evaluated according to the needs and risks of each application.

**Audio and transitions:**
- Use consistent icons or short animations for each application.
- Keep each example on screen long enough to read.
- Transition to the risks and responsibilities associated with AI.

**Production notes:**
- Avoid claiming that AI replaces teachers, healthcare professionals, or other qualified workers.
- Use appropriate, non-sensitive example images.
- Ensure the examples are understandable to beginners.

---

### Scene 6: Responsible AI

**Time:** 02:45–03:35  
**Duration:** 50 seconds

**Visuals:**
Present four key considerations using simple icons and short examples.

**1. Accuracy**
- Show a generated answer being checked against a reliable source.

**2. Bias and fairness**
- Show how unbalanced training data can contribute to unfair results.

**3. Privacy**
- Show a warning not to enter passwords, private records, or confidential information into an unapproved tool.

**4. Human oversight**
- Show a person reviewing an AI-generated recommendation before acting on it.

**Narration:**

"Using AI responsibly means understanding both its benefits and its risks. First, check important claims against reliable sources because AI-generated answers can be inaccurate. Second, consider bias: systems can produce unfair results when their data or design disadvantages certain groups. Third, protect privacy by avoiding the unnecessary sharing of personal or confidential information. Finally, keep people involved in important decisions. AI can provide assistance, but people must evaluate whether its outputs are appropriate for the situation."

**On-screen text:**
- Verify information.
- Question bias.
- Protect privacy.
- Keep human oversight.

**Learning point:**

Responsible AI use requires critical thinking, privacy awareness, fairness considerations, and appropriate human oversight.

**Audio and transitions:**
- Introduce each principle separately.
- Use clear visual symbols to reinforce the four points.
- Reduce the background music slightly during narration.

**Production notes:**
- Use realistic examples without exposing anyone's personal information.
- Avoid suggesting that a single check can eliminate all bias or risk.
- Ensure that the advice is practical for students and beginners.

---

**Time:** 03:35–04:10

**Visuals:**
- Return to the main concepts introduced in the video.

Machine learning is an approach that learns patterns from data, while generative AI creates content such as text and images. AI can support education, healthcare, transportation, and many other activities. But using it well requires us to verify information, consider fairness, protect privacy, and apply human judgement. Stay curious, ask questions, and use AI responsibly."

**On-screen text:**

- Check its outputs.
- Protect privacy.
- Use AI responsibly.

**Closing text:**

"Curious Mind — Learn, Question, Discover."

**Audio and transitions:**
- Fade in the closing title.
- Fade out the music at the end.
- Include credits and required acknowledgements if applicable.

**Production notes:**
- Make the closing message readable.
- Include any required course, team, or source credits.

## 4. Visual Design Guidelines

Use a consistent visual style throughout the video.

- **Layout:** Keep each scene simple and avoid overcrowding the screen.
- **Typography:** Use clear, readable fonts.
- **Text:** Show key phrases rather than long paragraphs.
- **Graphics:** Prefer simple diagrams, icons, and examples that reinforce the narration.
- **Transitions:** Use consistent transitions and avoid distracting effects.
- **Accessibility:** Add accurate captions and ensure that text has sufficient contrast.
- **Audio:** Keep narration clear and background music quiet.
- **Sources:** Credit third-party images, music, and other assets according to their licence and the course requirements.
